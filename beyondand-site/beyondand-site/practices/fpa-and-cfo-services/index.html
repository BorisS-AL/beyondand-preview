FPnA & Sponsor Reporting.

The board pack and the operating plan should be the same document. We make them the same document. Director-level FP&A capacity on US hours, driver-based modelling, sponsor-grade scenario and sensitivity built in.

The senior-analyst supply problem is real, and most CFOs are quietly funding their way around it. Director-level FP&A talent costs more than the US market currently allows; the bench between "an analyst built a model" and "a partner who can defend it to the board" is thinner than it was five years ago.

The conjunction puts director-level capacity on US hours at sponsor-tolerable pricing. Driver-based models the ops team actually runs from. Board packs that reconcile to the operating plan because they came out of the same model. Sponsor scenarios — downside, base, sponsor-case — sitting alongside the operating plan, not in a separate deck.

The deliverable is a model your audit committee can read and your sponsor can stress-test. Same numbers, same source, same partner on the call.

What lands here.

  • Director-level FP&A capacity. Senior analytical capacity, US hours, sponsor-grade pricing.
  • Driver-based modelling. The model the ops team actually runs from.
  • Sponsor-grade reporting. LP letter, board pack, audit-committee pre-read — one source of numbers.
  • Scenario & sensitivity. Downside, base, sponsor-case. Stress-tested before the sponsor asks.
  • Operating-plan integration. FP&A that reconciles to the close.
  • 13-week cash forecast. Weekly reforecast, variance explained, not asserted.

The senior-analyst shortage is real. The shortage of senior-analyst deliverables doesn't have to be.
