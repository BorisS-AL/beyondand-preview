01 — Practice

Close & Disclose.

Close is the wrong place to start a transformation. Disclosure is. We work backwards from the audit you're going to have and re-architect the close so the work product feeds the audit, not the other way around.

The teams that struggle with the close are the same teams whose auditors return with questions that should have been answered before the file was even built. Close and disclose, end to end, as one motion — not two unrelated calendars.

21+ days to under 10 is the typical landing zone. Audit prep stops being a Q4 fire drill and becomes the byproduct of a well-built close. Workpapers and evidence trails exist because the close produced them, not because someone built a separate audit binder.

Senior controllers embedded, named BRM on the engagement, partner accountable on the audit-committee report. Big-4-grade methodology in the work; sponsor-grade pricing in the bill.

What lands here.

  • Close acceleration. Month-end and quarter-end cycle compression. 21+ days to under 10.
  • Multi-entity consolidation. Intercompany elimination, FX translation, automated rollups.
  • Statement preparation. US GAAP and IFRS. ASC 606, 842, 350 memos defended in audit.
  • Audit-readiness as standard. Workpapers built into the close, not retrofitted in Q4.
  • Continuous-close architecture. Daily flux, materiality thresholds set right, no end-of-month panic.
  • Named BRM accountability. Single point of escalation; inside the cycle, not adjacent to it.

The close stopped being the controllership's worst week. Audit prep ran in the background, not in Q4.

Headline outcome through this conjunction.
21+ → <10
Day month-end close, audit-readiness built in. Multi-site healthcare provider engagement.
