Close to disclose · POV 01

Why the close is the wrong place to start a transformation.

Most close-acceleration projects fail in the same place: they start at the close. Days come off the cycle. Then they come back at audit time.

The mistake is structural, not technical. The close exists to produce the financial statements; the statements exist to support the audit and the disclosures. When the close is treated as an operational metric to be optimized, the optimization tends to trade evidence quality for speed.

The fix is to start at the other end. The 10-K. The footnotes. The prior-year audit committee report. The technical accounting memos the audit firm asked for last cycle. Work backwards from those documents, and the close shape that emerges is one where the workpapers are produced as a byproduct — not as a Q4 retrofit.

What it looks like in practice: a calendar your audit committee can read. Daily flux instead of end-of-month panic. Workpapers and evidence trails that exist because the close produced them, not because someone built a separate audit binder. The auditor stops asking the same five questions every quarter, because the answers are already in the file by the time the question lands.

The conjunction here is close & disclose. Buy them together. Build them together. The work product is one motion.

Don't optimize the close. Optimize the disclosure the close feeds. The close fixes itself.
