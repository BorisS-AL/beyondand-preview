FP&A · POV 03

The FP&A supply problem is real — and it's the wrong problem.

Every CFO has the same complaint about FP&A: they can't hire the senior analytical talent they need at a price the operating budget can carry. The complaint is structurally accurate. It's also the wrong problem to solve.

The supply problem is real. Director-level FP&A talent has gotten genuinely expensive faster than middle-market operating budgets have grown. The bench between "an analyst built a model" and "a partner who can defend it to the board" is thinner than it was five years ago.

The wrong response is to keep hunting for them. The right response is to ask what those analysts actually produce. In most cases the answer is: a driver-based operating model, a board pack that reconciles to the operating model, sponsor scenarios that stress-test both, and a 13-week cash forecast the CFO can sign their name to.

Four artifacts. The director-level analyst is the production mechanism. If the production mechanism can be staffed by a director-level analyst sitting inside another firm, on US hours, at a price the operating budget can carry — the supply problem disappears. The deliverables show up. The CFO signs their name to the cash forecast.

The conjunction here is FP&A & sponsor reporting. Director-level capacity on US hours; the board pack and the LP letter coming out of the same model. That's what the Beyond& bench is configured to do.

The senior-analyst shortage is real. The shortage of senior-analyst deliverables doesn't have to be.
